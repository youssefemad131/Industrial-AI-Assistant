EVIDENCE BEFORE CONCLUSIONS

FactoryLens AI is a discussion prototype for factory engineers. It helps structure a quality investigation by separating recorded facts, possible explanations, user-reported observations, and missing data—without presenting a hypothesis as a proven cause.

Journey map · Concept B

Example journey; validate it with users rather than treating it as observed research.

1 · Start

User selects a question or suggested issue. Need: know what the tool covers.

2 · Clarify

Assistant asks for a time period when needed. Need: minimal, relevant questions.

3 · Understand

User reviews facts, hypotheses, and missing data. Need: visible sources and uncertainty.

4 · Verify

User chooses a batch check or maintenance check. Need: keep human decision authority.

5 · Finish

User records the check and reassesses. Need: clear handoff and no unsupported conclusion.

Bias and inclusion checks

• Do not infer skill from age, nationality, accent, or job title; ask what help the user wants.

• Keep equivalent evidence and safety warnings across personas and language settings.

• Test readability, keyboard access, mobile layouts, and terminology with different users.

• The fictional personas represent varied AI familiarity and language/access needs; they do not prove the design is unbiased.

Human–AI Interaction Principle Ladder

Each rung adds a stronger form of user understanding or control. These are design commitments to evaluate, not claims of validated performance.

01 · INFORM

Label what is shown

Mark facts, hypotheses, user reports, missing data, and illustrative content distinctly.

Measure: correct evidence-category identification.

02 · EXPLAIN

Show why

Provide source, time range, and reason for a hypothesis; say what evidence is absent.

Measure: source-tracing success.

03 · ALLOW CONTROL

Let people choose

Ask clarification when needed and let the user choose the next verification step.

Measure: task completion and perceived control.

04 · ENABLE FEEDBACK

Support correction

Make it possible to report a wrong assumption or missing record and reassess the answer.

Measure: feedback completion and successful correction rate.

Proposed evaluation metrics

Targets below are proposed test thresholds, not achieved results. Measure with representative users and recorded test tasks.

≥ 90%
Evidence-label comprehension

Share of test users correctly distinguishing fact, hypothesis, and missing data.

≥ 85%
Source tracing

Share who can locate the source/time window for a displayed fact.

≥ 80%
Task completion

Share completing a defined investigation task without facilitator intervention.

≤ 1
Unsupported causal claims

Maximum per 20 scripted test cases; goal is zero definitive causes without adequate evidence.

≥ 4/5
Perceived user control

Average rating after the test: “I could choose and verify the next step.”

≥ 90%
Feedback completion

Share of users who can submit a correction/missing-data report in a usability test.

Discussion guide for the project review

Use these prompts to explain the design choices and invite critique. The demo is a concept prototype, not evidence that the proposed metrics have been met.

1. Problem and users

Say: “FactoryLens AI supports structured investigation of manufacturing quality issues. It is designed for users with different levels of AI experience.”

Ask: “Which user needs or factory constraints are missing from these personas?”

2. Compare the concepts

Show: ask “Why did paint defects increase?” in Concept A, then switch to Concept B.

Explain: Concept A provides a direct analysis; Concept B asks for the comparison period and lets the user select a verification step.

Ask: “Does the clarification help, or does it add unnecessary friction?”

3. Trust and evidence

Show: the green fact block, amber hypothesis block, and grey missing-data block.

Explain: a timing match is not proof of causation; missing measurements should remain visible.

Ask: “What source details would an engineer need before acting?”

4. Human control and safety

Explain: the assistant suggests checks; it does not authorize machine changes or replace quality procedures.

Ask: “Which actions should require approval, a second check, or escalation?”

5. Evaluation and next steps

Explain: metrics are targets awaiting testing. A future evaluation should use synthetic scenarios first, then approved and privacy-reviewed factory data.

Ask: “Which metric best demonstrates value without hiding safety or usability problems?”

Important demo wording: “The figures and records are illustrative. The prototype uses scripted responses and is not connected to a live factory system. The metrics are proposed targets, not measured results.”
